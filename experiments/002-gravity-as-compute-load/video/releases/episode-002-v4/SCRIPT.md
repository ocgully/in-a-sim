> Here's the Earth. Picture everything heavy sitting in a dip. Now put one clock up on the rim, and one down near the Earth. The lower clock ticks slower. Not because it's broken, but because time itself runs slower near something heavy. That dip isn't a hole in space. It's a map: the deeper you are, the slower your time runs. Physicists have measured this with clocks a millimetre apart. Over a lifetime, your feet age about half a millionth of a second less than your head.

## 6. ripple

**On screen:** tiles_ripple (play), tiles_ripple (hold)  
**HUD:** EVERY TILE IS A CLOCK · violet = slower · idea: Whitworth (2008), Alagoz (2010) · we built it

> In a game, a region full of stuff costs more to compute, so it updates slower. What if that's what mass does? The slowdown spreads outward and fades with distance, in exactly the shape Newton found for gravity. And the slower the tiles, the deeper the dip. This idea isn't new. Brian Whitworth and Alagoz suggested it years ago. We wanted to build it and see if it holds up.

## 14. sync

**On screen:** sync_space (play), sync_space (hold)  
**HUD:** A HYPOTHESIS · the tick is a signal · more tiles to cross → slower tick → 1 : 1 · our speculation · not established physics

> And measurements say the extra space and the extra lag always come in exactly equal amounts, to two parts in a hundred thousand. Here's one way an engine would get that for free. If every tick is a signal that has to travel across its tiles, then more tiles means a slower tick. More space and more lag, always one for one.
